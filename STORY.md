# The Story Behind This

April 2nd, 2025. The Egyptian Armed Forces took me. Just like that. No negotiation,
no review, no appeal. One day I was a person with plans, the next I was a number
in a queue, staring at a wall, counting days instead of living them.

But before the countdown even started, there was the waiting game. From the very
first day of enrollment, there were examinations. Then interviews. Then results that
only told you whether you were allowed to move to the next phase, not whether you
were done. Each round was a door that either opened or shut in your face, and you
wouldn't know which until they called your name. Or didn't. You'd pass one stage,
exhale for a day, then realize there was another stage behind it. This went on for
months. A long, slow, draining process of proving yourself to something that had
already decided it owned your time.

October 27th, 2025 was the last of it. The final results day. The day that would
tell you whether you were going to the Faculty of Reserve Officers or not.

---

A sergeant looked me in the eye and called out my name in public — telling me I had
passed all the examinations and interviews and that I was going to the Faculty of
Reserve Officers next week. In that exact moment, the whole world went black.
Everything I had calculated, every plan I had quietly built in my head: gone. Just
darkness and the sound of my own thoughts collapsing.

Then, from somewhere underneath all of it, I found myself saying: *Alhamdulellah.*

Not because it was good news. Not because I understood it. But because some things
are bigger than your plans, and the only honest response to that is surrender.
Grateful surrender. The weeks that followed were pure brainfuck, chaos dressed up
in a uniform, rules that existed to break you, schedules that changed just to remind
you that you were not in charge. The red on the bar is that whole era. It earned
every pixel.

Then on January 11th, 2026, I was downgraded to a soldier. I stayed four more days
in that cursed place — and the moment I walked out, I couldn't believe it was real.
What followed was around 45 days as a soldier. Lighter, somehow. Random in the best
and worst ways, a laugh in the afternoons & nights you didn't see coming, a silly moment
that made the whole thing feel almost human. Not easy. Just... livable.

And then the current days at the current camp. Heavy-hearted and systematic. No
chaos anymore just precision. Same time, same place, same weight sitting on your
chest every morning. The routine doesn't break you loudly. It does it quietly,
consistently, every single day. This is the long blue stretch. This is where we are.

The white at the end of the bar is everything still owed. December 1st, 2026 sits
behind a 🏁 — the racing finish flag for a very tired horse ready for a new chapter.
